1 · Before you buy

What you're building

A SkyGlass mirror is a shallow wooden box. The front is two-way mirror glass. Behind it, a computer monitor sits face-down against the glass, with a Raspberry Pi and a small camera alongside. Where the screen is lit, its light shows through the glass; everywhere else you see an ordinary mirror. The box hangs on a French cleat and runs from one wall outlet.

Decide four things first

01

Display size

Everything else is sized from the display. 24" is the sweet spot: easy to read from across a room and light enough to hang. Most builds use 22–27".

02

Landscape or portrait

Landscape suits a hallway or desk. Portrait (display turned 90°) makes a taller mirror for a dressing area. The parts are the same; only the glass and frame dimensions change.

03

Glass or acrylic

Glass gives a true, flat reflection and is the recommendation. Two-way acrylic is cheaper and lighter but bends the reflection on larger sizes. See the comparison on page 6.

04

Where it hangs, and how power gets there

The finished 24" mirror weighs about 30–35 lb. Plan to screw into a wall stud, and decide whether the power cord shows, runs in a cord cover, or goes through the wall.

Measure your display before you order glass. Record the body width and height (stand removed), the depth at its thickest point, the size of the lit screen area, the VESA hole spacing, and where the HDMI and power ports are. Glass is cut to order and usually can't be returned.

What to ask the glass shop for

Two-way (transparent) mirror glass, 6 mm (¼"), cut to 24" × 18".
Seamed edges, please. Mark which side is the coated side.
It's for a mirror with a screen behind it, so I need a clear view through it from the back.

Ask which side should face the room for their glass, and mark that side with tape before you handle it. For tempered glass, order it at the final size: tempered glass can't be cut afterwards.

Not building a frame?

  • Picture-framing shop: ask for a deep "shadow box" or a frame with a build-up (riser) giving at least 3½" inside depth, and bring your glass. Expect $150–300 instead of E1–E10.
  • Off-the-shelf shadow boxes are almost always too shallow for a monitor. Check the inside depth, not the outside depth.
  • 3D printing: printed corner brackets can join aluminum or wood rails, but you still need a back panel strong enough for the cleat.

Any other size

With glass W × H:

  • Inside of box = (W + ⅛") × (H + ⅛")
  • Box top + bottom = inside width + 1½"
  • Box sides = inside height
  • Front frame and back panel = outside size: inside + 1½" each way
  • Visible mirror = inside − 1½" each way
  • Long stops = inside width; short stops = inside height − 1"

Layout tips that affect what you buy

  • Place the display high. Mount it about 2" below the inside top. That leaves room above it for the camera at eye level, and a strip about 3½" tall below it for the Pi, the power strip and the monitor's power brick.
  • Ventilate. Drill four or five 1" holes through the top rail and the same through the bottom rail (or the back panel near the top and bottom). Heat rises out of the top and cool air comes in at the bottom.
  • Keep the controls reachable. Cut a finger hole in the back panel over the monitor's power button, or set the monitor to switch on when power returns.
  • Camera behind the glass works in a normally lit room. For a dim room, drill a hole just larger than the lens through the top of the front frame and mount the camera there instead.
  • Portrait builds: turn the display 90° and switch the mirror to portrait in its display settings. Put the Pi in the space below the display.
4 · Tools

Tools and supplies

Tools aren't included in the parts totals. Most home centers rent miter saws, or will make straight cuts for you at the lumber counter.

For every build

  • Tape measure and pencil
  • Combination or speed square
  • Phillips and flat screwdrivers
  • Utility knife and a metal straightedge (for the mask)
  • Stud finder
  • 4 ft or 2 ft level
  • Work gloves (for glass) and safety glasses
  • Computer with an SD card slot (or reader A10)
  • iPhone or Android phone with the SkyGlass app

For the wooden frame

  • Miter saw, or a hand saw with a miter box
  • Drill/driver with bits: ⅛" pilot, countersink, 1" spade or hole saw
  • Four corner clamps or two bar clamps
  • Brad nailer, or a hammer and nail set
  • Sanding block or random-orbit sander
  • Paint brush, small roller or spray cans
  • Optional: table or circular saw (for the plywood cleat), pocket-hole jig
